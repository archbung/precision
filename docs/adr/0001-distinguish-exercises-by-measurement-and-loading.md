# Distinguish exercises by measurement mode, equipment, and load convention

An exercise has one fixed primary measurement mode and load convention, so sled push for distance and sled push for duration are separate exercises. We chose separate exercises over multiple primary measurement modes within one exercise to simplify inspecting history, accepting a larger exercise catalog. Bodyweight, weighted, and assisted pull-ups share an exercise using signed added bodyweight load; equipment differences such as bands versus machines still distinguish exercises.

Different equipment types distinguish exercises, while individual machines of the same equipment type are grouped together for now. Once recorded history exists, measurement mode and load convention remain fixed; changing either creates another exercise, while genuine mistakes require explicit correction.

External load means total external weight, including the barbell, sled, or both dumbbells when applicable. Exercise names are unique ignoring case and communicate distinctions such as measurement mode; names may be clarified, but changed technique or defining equipment creates another exercise. Equipment references describe the loading setup rather than every item required: a bench need not be referenced for bench press.

An exercise may reference zero or several equipment types: a band-assisted pull-up references Band, while a barbell squat with bands references both Barbell and Band. Cable and Machine are distinct types. Optional muscle-group references distinguish primary targets from secondary supporting groups.

An exercise has at most one optional primary muscle group and zero or more secondary groups, with no overlap; secondary groups may be assigned without a primary group. Fullbody is available for whole-body exercises. The initial equipment catalog contains Barbell, Dumbbell, Kettlebell, Band, Cable, Machine, Sled, and Weight Plate. The initial muscle-group catalog contains Chest, Back, Shoulders, Biceps, Triceps, Forearms, Abdominals, Glutes, Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Calves, and Fullbody. These bundled catalogs may be extended as concrete exercises require.

The MVP is a CLI backed by SQLite with user-defined exercises. New databases now receive a starter catalog of 60 exercises captured from the user’s database in migration 0008, so a fresh installation does not require re-entering them. These are ordinary editable exercises, with no ongoing synchronization; existing databases retain their own catalogs and identities. This supersedes the initial decision to supply no exercise catalog. Equipment types and muscle groups come from bundled catalogs that users cannot extend. The initially deferred set and workout organisation is resolved in [ADR 0002](0002-record-sets-as-ordered-exercise-portions.md): a performance is a performed set with one or more exercise portions, each with one primary measurement. Optional kilograms and load description belong to the set; unknown kilograms remain unspecified, and secondary measurements may be recorded in a note. History presents recorded performances without estimating equivalence or performance, or judging improvement across exercises or load setups. Exercise families, structured technique descriptions, movement patterns, and joint actions remain outside scope.
